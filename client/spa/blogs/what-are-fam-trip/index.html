What Are Fam Trips? A Guide for Destinations

Fam trips are hosted visits that a destination, hotel, airline, or cruise line organises for the people who sell or promote it, such as travel agents, tour operators, MICE planners, journalists and creators. The idea is simple: people sell a place with more conviction once they have been there themselves.

If you're wondering what FAM means in the travel industry, it's short for familiarisation, which is why you'll also hear the terms familiarisation tour and, in the US, familiarization trip. The harder question for a tourism board or travel brand is whether a fam trip is worth the budget, and how to make it pay back in bookings.

Key takeaways

  • A fam trip is a hosted trip for trade partners, media or creators, designed to turn first-hand experience into sales or coverage.
  • Start with the business objective, then choose guests who can deliver it, not those with the biggest following or the most senior title.
  • The trip itself is only half the work. Follow-up in the first week decides the return.
  • Measure bookings, agent sales and search demand, not just impressions.

When a fam trip makes sense

A fam trip earns its cost when a market needs to see something new or see you differently. Good triggers include:

  • entering or re-entering the Indian outbound market, which recorded 32.7 million Indian national departures in 2025 according to the Ministry of Tourism's Annual Tourism Snapshot
  • launching a new air route, property, cruise product or campaign
  • changing perception, for example moving from a honeymoon-only image to a family or adventure destination
  • building demand for a shoulder season

If none of these apply, a trade webinar or a sales roadshow may do the job for less money.

Types of fam trips

People often ask what a fam trip is in tourism because the term covers a few different formats. All of them are hosted, but they differ in who travels and what the host gets back.

  • Trade fam trip: hosts travel agents, tour operators and DMCs. The return is product knowledge, new packages and bookings.
  • Media or press trip: hosts journalists and editors. The return is earned coverage.
  • Influencer trip: hosts creators. The return is social content and reach.
  • MICE fam trip: hosts corporate travel managers and event planners. The return is group and incentive business.

Many tourism boards run several formats across a year, but mixing them in one group rarely works. Agents want rate sheets and supplier meetings, whereas creators need unhurried time and good light to produce content.

Who to invite on a fam trip, based on your goal

Build the guest list backwards from the result you need.

  • For leisure bookings, invite travel agents and tour operators from key Indian source cities, covering Tier 2 cities as well as the metros.
  • For group, wedding or MICE business, invite corporate travel managers, MICE planners and destination wedding planners.
  • For awareness and search demand, invite travel journalists and creators whose audience matches your target traveller. Our guide to travel influencer marketing covers how to vet creators.
  • For stronger distribution, invite DMCs, OTA market managers and airline sales teams.

Qualify every trade guest before you confirm them. Ask what they sold last year, which destinations they sell today and who in their team makes the booking decisions.

Building an itinerary that sells

A fam trip usually involves travel, hotel stays, guided experiences and meetings, but the mix matters more than the volume. Plan for:

  1. site inspections of the hotels and room categories guests will actually sell, not just the flagship suite
  2. experiences that suit Indian travellers, such as vegetarian and Jain food options, family activities, shopping and photo spots
  3. short B2B sessions with local DMCs and suppliers so agents leave with contacts and rates
  4. free time, because tired guests stop absorbing information by day three

A simple four-day structure could look like this:

  • Day 1: arrival, a welcome dinner and a short destination briefing
  • Day 2: the city product, including hotels, key attractions and shopping
  • Day 3: a signature experience day that could anchor a package
  • Day 4: supplier meetings, then departure with a sales kit in hand

Budget and cost-sharing

Attendees usually travel free, so the host carries the cost. Most fam trips follow one of three models:

  • the tourism board leads and funds the trip
  • airlines and hotels co-host, contributing seats and rooms in return for product exposure
  • a DMC partners on ground handling

Airline partnerships are especially useful for new routes. We look at this in more detail in airlines marketing with tourism boards. Spend on experiences and smooth local transport, and save on luxury upgrades that agents cannot sell.

Timing it around the Indian travel calendar

Plan fam trips so buyers arrive already interested. SATTE in New Delhi runs 19–21 January 2027, and OTM is held in Mumbai each February. Both are natural places to meet and shortlist guests. Our work with Zambia Tourism at OTM Mumbai 2026 shows how a trade show presence can open trade relationships for a first-time entrant.

Host trips ahead of India's main booking windows: summer school holidays, Diwali and the long weekends at the end of the year.

After the trip: turning visits into bookings

The days after guests fly home decide the return. Send each agent an exclusive offer or booking code valid for a limited window. Build co-branded content or landing pages for your strongest partners, and reuse creator and agent content on your own channels.

Paid amplification aimed at the Indian audiences the trip reached can extend the effect. When Costa Cruises introduced international cruising to India, more than 40 creators were hosted on board to experience the ship first-hand, which gave a new category real faces and real content.

How to measure whether a fam trip worked

Track:

  • bookings made with trip-specific codes
  • agent sales before and after the trip
  • earned media and content reach
  • growth in branded and destination searches from India

Tie these back to your wider destination marketing goals, so the fam trip is judged as part of a plan rather than a one-off event.

Frequently asked questions

What does FAM stand for in travel?

FAM stands for familiarisation. A fam trip, or familiarisation tour, is a hosted visit that helps travel sellers and media get to know a destination or product first-hand.

Are fam trips free for attendees?

Usually, yes. The host covers flights, stays, meals and activities, often with airline, hotel or DMC partners. Attendees give their time and commit to selling or covering the destination.

How often should a tourism board run a fam trip?

It depends on the goal and budget. Rather than working to a fixed number, plan fam trips around each key source market's trade shows and booking windows, so every trip has a clear sales purpose.
