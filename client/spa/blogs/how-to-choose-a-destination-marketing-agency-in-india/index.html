How to Choose a Destination Marketing Agency in India?

A destination marketing agency in India helps foreign tourism boards, DMOs, NTOs and travel brands win Indian travellers through trade engagement, PR, creators, activations and paid media. As to how one should choose such an agency, the advice is to select the one that has a thorough understanding of Indian audiences broken down segment by segment, already maintains contacts within the travel trade, collaborates with regional creators, has provided services for government clients and evaluates its success in terms of arrivals rather than reach.

The rest of this guide shows how to test each of those before you sign.

Key takeaways

What a destination marketing agency does for a tourism board

For an overseas tourism board, the agency is effectively its team on the ground. It plans the annual programme, runs trade roadshows and agent training, handles media relations and FAM trips and travel PR, manages creators and paid media, and reports results back to head office.

Some boards also appoint a market representative to run trade sales and day-to-day office functions. Others combine both roles in one contract, as Tourism Ireland's India tender did when it asked a single India-based supplier to deliver trade, publicity and marketing together. Settle which model you need first, because it changes who you invite to pitch.

Why India needs a specialist DMO marketing agency

It is obvious what the scope of the opportunity is. The Ministry of Tourism recorded 3.27 crore (32.7 million) Indian national departures in 2025 (provisional), up 5.89% on 2024. Reaching those travellers is harder than the headline suggests:

  • A first-time traveller with a family from Indore and one who frequently travels from Bengaluru plan and research their trips in different ways.
  • Discovery happens in more than one language. Google and Kantar's research found 41% of Indian travellers rely heavily on influencers when planning, and some research in Hindi, Tamil and Marathi.
  • Travel agents still influence a great many decisions, particularly when it comes to families, groups and cities in Tier 2.
  • Demand follows the Indian calendar. It rises during summer school holidays, Diwali, wedding season, and long weekends.

A generalist agency, or a global network running India from a regional hub, can miss most of this.

Six criteria to evaluate a destination marketing agency in India

1. Audience insight specific to India

Inquire into the way the agency would divide your audience and ask for the evidence it has for each of those segments. Singapore Tourism Board's #IsThisEvenSingapore campaign targeted young Indian travellers through five subcultures, and the under-35 share of Indian arrivals rose from 31% to 39%.

2. Existing trade relationships

The agency should already be familiar with tour operators, travel agents, MICE planners and OTA market managers, even if they are based outside the metropolitan areas. For a company new to the market, trade shows like OTM and SATTE are usually the point at which access to the market is gained. Zambia Tourism Authority's first appearance at OTM Mumbai 2026 won Most Promising Destination, and the authority reported more than 500 trade connections over its one-year mandate.

3. A regional creator network

English-language creators in Mumbai and Delhi reach only part of the market. Ask which regional-language creators the agency has worked with and what their content produced. For Resorts World Sentosa, a creator campaign on ShareChat and Moj across 15 Indian states delivered 4,927 direct bookings through a tracked link. Our guide to travel influencer marketing covers how to vet creators.

4. Experience reporting to government clients

Because tourism boards report to ministries, the agency must operate within the approval procedures, procurement rules, and reporting formats set by head office. Request a sample monthly report prepared for a government client, and identify who is responsible for the embassy, airline, and hotel partners.

5. Measurement tied to arrivals

Reach and engagement can be useful when assessing a campaign, but a ministry is interested in finding out what actually caused people to take action. Reliable agencies agree to set up KPIs such as the number of arrivals from India, the number of visa applications, agent sales, bookings on tracked codes and branded search demand, each with a baseline prior to the launch.

6. Range across trade, consumer and content

One team running trade, consumer and content work keeps the message consistent. Check the agency can handle experiential marketing, such as the mall activation for Saudi tourism that showed Indian families Saudi Arabia's leisure side, and longer-term branded content and IP like Singapore Tourism Board's partnership with Dharma Productions on Jigra.

Questions to ask in the pitch or RFP

  1. Which destinations do you currently represent, and do any compete with us? Some boards make this a rule: the Philippines' Tourism Promotions Board barred agencies already working for other ASEAN or Asian tourism organisations from bidding for its India mandate.
  2. Who will run our account day-to-day, and what India travel experience do they have?
  3. Can you show a case with Frequently asked questionsan arrivals or bookings result, and explain how it was measured?
  4. Which Tier 2 trade partners could you bring to our next roadshow?
  5. Which regional-language creators have you worked with, and on what?
  6. What would your first monthly report to our head office look like?
  7. Who owns the ad accounts, content, and creator usage rights when the contract ends?

Red flags to watch for

  • Case studies that report only impressions, followers or views
  • Creators chosen by follower count, or the same roster offered to every client
  • No trade contacts outside Mumbai and Delhi
  • An undisclosed contract with a competing destination
  • A senior pitch team with no named role in delivery
  • Guaranteed arrival numbers, which no agency can honestly promise

Choosing the right partner

The right destination marketing agency in India will show you segment-level insight, real trade and creator relationships, and a plan to report arrivals rather than reach. Frequently asked questions

What does a destination marketing agency in India do?

It plans and runs a destination's marketing in the Indian market, including trade engagement, PR, FAM trips, creator programmes, activations, paid media and reporting to the tourism board's head office.

What is the difference between a DMO marketing agency and a market representative?

Normally, a market representative looks after the trade sales and the office duties for a tourism board, whereas a DMO marketing agency carries out the campaigns, public relations, content production and employs creators. In some cases, the boards appoint a single partner to take on both roles.

How much does a destination marketing agency in India cost?

It depends on scope, the number of cities, trade events, creator volume, and media spend. Ask every bidder to separate management fees, production, creator fees and media so proposals can be compared line by line.

Should a tourism board choose an India-based agency or a global network?

A global network can help with consistency across markets, but an India-based specialist usually brings deeper trade relationships, regional creator access, and knowledge of the Indian travel calendar. Many boards test this by asking both for city-level case studies.

How should a tourism board measure its agency?

Agree on one main KPI per goal before launch, with a baseline. Useful measures include arrivals from India, visa applications, agent sales, bookings on tracked codes, and branded search demand.
