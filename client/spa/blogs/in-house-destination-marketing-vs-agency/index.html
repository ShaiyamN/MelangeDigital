Full-Service, Specialist or In-House: Which Destination Marketing Setup Should a Tourism Board Choose for India?

A tourism board marketing to Indian travellers can hire a full-service destination marketing agency, appoint a lead agency that brings in specialist partners, or build an in-house team, often supported by a market representative. When it comes to in-house destination marketing vs agency support, the right choice depends on how new you are to India, how many disciplines you need at once, and whether you can report campaign activity against arrivals.

The market is worth getting right. The Ministry of Tourism's data dashboard records 32.83 million Indian national departures in 2025, up 6.3 per cent on the previous year.

When does a tourism board need an agency in India?

For most boards, the case for hiring a destination marketing agency vs. in-house resourcing comes down to capacity: outside help comes in when the work outgrows the team available to do it. The usual triggers are:

  • First-time market entry: no trade relationships, creator shortlist or baseline data yet.
  • A campaign launch: creators, media, trade and content need to land together, often around a trade fair or new air route.
  • No team on the ground: casting creators, hosting media and following up with operators needs people in India.
  • Government reporting: ministries expect results in arrivals, spend and trade terms.

What are the three setups?

  • Full-service integrated destination marketing agency: one team owns strategy, creator casting, PR and FAM trips, content, trade engagement, and reporting.
  • Lead agency with specialist partners: a lead agency sets strategy and coordinates specialist firms, such as a creator agency, PR firm, or production house.
  • In-house team, with or without a market representative: your own marketers run campaigns. A market representative typically handles trade liaison and trade fairs, but rarely runs consumer campaigns.

What should you compare?

Five capabilities separate real India depth from a strong pitch:

  • Regional creator casting: audiences in Chennai, Hyderabad or Kolkata often respond better to creators who speak their language than to metro English-language influencers. Ask whether creators are cast by audience fit or follower count.
  • ASCI disclosure compliance: the Advertising Standards Council of India's influencer guidelines require a label such as Ad, Sponsored or Collaboration whenever there is a material connection, and free perks such as a hosted trip count. On videos of 15 seconds or less, the label must stay on screen for at least 3 seconds. Advertisers share responsibility, so disclosure belongs in every brief and contract.
  • PR and FAM trip depth: strong FAM trips and PR mix trade media with lifestyle press, and build itineraries around what Indian travellers actually book.
  • Trade relationships: outbound operators and agents remain an important channel, particularly for packaged and group holidays. Look for named relationships and experience at OTM Mumbai and SATTE Delhi.
  • Measurement tied to arrivals: ask for reporting that links activity to visa applications, flight searches, trade leads and arrivals, against a baseline agreed before launch.

How do the three setups compare?

Full-service integrated destination marketing agency

  • Works when: you are entering India for the first time, want one accountable partner, and need creators, PR and trade telling one story with one report.
  • Falls short when: the agency is strong in one discipline and thin in the rest, or you need specialist production outside its core team.

Lead agency with specialist partners

  • Works when: you already have an India strategy and need extra depth in one or two disciplines.
  • Falls short when: partners report separately, handoffs slow down time-bound launches, and accountability is split.

In-house marketing team, with or without a market representative

  • Works when: you have an established India presence, local staff, and a steady programme, and want full control of relationships and data.
  • Falls short when: creator networks and trade relationships must be built from scratch, or the team is too small to run creators, media, trade and reporting at once.

What should you ask before you appoint anyone?

  • "What arrivals-linked results have you delivered for a tourism board?" Red flag: only likes, reach and views.
  • "How do you handle ASCI disclosure on hosted creator trips?" Red flag: disclosure left to the creator.
  • "Which trade relationships will you use for us?" Red flag: a vague "network" with no names.

For a fuller list of criteria, pricing models and red flags, see our guide on how to choose a destination marketing agency in India.

Which setup should you choose?

  • Full-service integrated agency if you are entering India for the first time, launching a major campaign, or need one partner accountable for arrivals.
  • Lead agency with specialist partners if you have an India strategy and need depth in one or two disciplines.
  • In-house team with a market representative if you have an established India presence and want full control.

The in-house destination marketing vs agency decision is rarely permanent. Many boards move between these setups over time: full-service at market entry, then a lighter setup once relationships and reporting are in place.

Frequently asked questions

Can an in-house team handle the Indian market?

Yes, if the destination already has local staff, trade relationships and a steady programme. Starting from zero is harder, so when weighing up hiring a destination marketing agency vs in-house teams, most first-time entrants work with an agency first and bring work in-house later.

Do hosted influencer trips have to be disclosed as ads in India?

Yes. Under ASCI's guidelines, a free trip creates a material connection, so the content needs a disclosure label. A creator who travels at their own expense has no material connection.
