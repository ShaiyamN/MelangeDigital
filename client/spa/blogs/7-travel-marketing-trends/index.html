7 Travel Marketing Trends 2027

Travel marketing trends in 2027 come down to three shifts: how travellers discover destinations, how campaigns prove their impact, and how marketers reach distinct audience segments. AI assistants now sit alongside search, video and creators as a way to shortlist destinations. Meanwhile, marketers face growing pressure to link awareness to searches, bookings and arrivals.

Demand is healthy. UN Tourism estimates 1.52 billion international arrivals in 2025, up 4%, with 3% to 4% growth expected in 2026. The fight is over who gets considered.

Key Takeaways

  • AI assistants are joining search, video and creators as a channel travellers use to shortlist destinations.
  • Long-term creator partnerships, including regional-language creators, build more credibility than one-off sponsored posts.
  • Marketers need to link awareness and consideration metrics to destination searches, bookings and arrivals.
  • Destinations targeting Indian travellers should tailor messaging to each segment.

Why Destination Marketing Trends Matter for Tourism Boards and DMOs

For tourism boards, DMOs or NTOs, these destination marketing trends change three things:

  • Budgets face harder questions. Stakeholders want to see how a campaign’s reach and engagement translated into searches, bookings and arrivals.
  • Source markets are fragmenting. A first-time traveller from Indore and a frequent flyer from Bengaluru won't respond to the same campaign.
  • Discovery is scattered across AI tools, YouTube, OTT and creators, so traditional media plans miss much of it.

A destination marketing plan for 2027 that copies the 2023 plan will reach fewer of the right travellers.

7 Emerging Trends in Tourism Marketing for 2027

1. AI Assistants Become a New Discovery Channel

More travellers now use ChatGPT, Gemini or Google’s AI Overviews alongside search, YouTube and creators to shortlist destinations. Phocuswright's 2025 research found 33% of US travellers already use generative AI in the travel process. These tools cite pages that answer specific questions clearly: best months, visa rules, costs. See our guide to AI for travel planning.

What to do: Build question-led pages, add structured data, and check what AI assistants say about your destination.

2. Creator Marketing Moves to Long-Term Partnerships

Travellers are becoming more selective about sponsored content, and a single sponsored post rarely shifts a booking. Creators still carry real weight: Google and Kantar research found 41% of Indian travellers rely heavily on influencers during trip planning. Long-term partnerships and audience relevance help destinations build that credibility, because creators who return to a destination over time come across as more believable. Some segments also research in Hindi, Tamil and Marathi. 

What to do: Build an influencer marketing roster with regional-language creators, chosen for audience fit over follower count.

3. FAM Trips Go Hybrid

More destinations are now bringing tour operators, journalists and creators together on one itinerary, but with separate sessions for each. One trip delivers trade training, earned media and social media content.

What to do: Plan FAM trips and travel PR around shared outcomes you can report against one budget.

4. Film, OTT and Branded Content Drive Destination Choice

Indian audiences have long travelled to places they first saw on screen. The shift is from paying for a shoot to co-creating content IP, as with Singapore Tourism Board's partnership with Dharma Productions for Jigra. In Film-induced tourism, travellers go to see the locations where the films were shot, to reenact a scene or to stay in the places where the characters did.

What to do: Treat branded content and IP as a multi-year asset.

5. Experiential Activations Move Into Source Markets

Destinations are taking the experience to consumers’ home cities through mall activations, pop-ups and cultural festivals. The mall activation for Saudi tourism tackled the Indian view of Saudi Arabia as mainly a religious destination.

What to do: Use experiential marketing to shift one specific perception, and capture leads on site.

6. Measurement Connects Awareness to Arrivals

Reach and engagement still matter, since creators, film and activations build awareness long before anyone books. What has changed is the pressure to connect those metrics with measurable outcomes: destination searches, bookings, visa applications and arrivals. OTA co-marketing with booking data, tracked landing pages and brand lift studies help draw that line. For tourism boards, a word like “sustainable” on a campaign page now needs evidence behind it. A specific fact is also more convincing to a traveller than a vague promise.

What to do: Agree metrics before launch, including at least one tied to bookings.

7. Sustainability Claims Need Proof

From 27 September 2026, the EU's Empowering Consumers Directive bans generic claims like "eco-friendly" unless substantiated, which affects any destination marketing to Europeans.Sustainability labels must also come from a recognised certification scheme or public authority. This applies to any destination marketing to European travellers, even from outside the EU.

What to do: Replace vague green language with verifiable facts.

What These Trends Mean for the Indian Outbound Market

India shows many new trends in tourism marketing in action. The Ministry of Tourism recorded 3.27 crore Indian national departures in 2025 (provisional), up 5.89% on 2024. Segments to plan for separately:

  • Weddings and MICE: large groups and long planning cycles.
  • Gen Z and solo travellers: creator-led, like the under-35 audience behind Melange's #IsThisEvenSingapore campaign.
  • Families: value-led, planning around school holidays.
  • Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities: first-time travellers who rely on regional-language content and travel agents.

For destinations new to India, trade presence still matters, as with Zambia Tourism's first appearance at OTM Mumbai 2026

How to Prioritise These Trends in Your 2027 Plan

  1. Quick wins (0–3 months): AI-ready content, a creator shortlist and agreed metrics.
  2. Mid-term (3–9 months): A hybrid FAM trip, OTA co-marketing and one source-market activation.
  3. Long-term (9–18 months): Film or branded content IP and long-term creator partnerships.

Conclusion

Travel marketing trends 2027 point one way: reach each traveller segment on the channels where it researches, then show how that awareness turns into searches, bookings and arrivals. See our work to learn more.

FAQs

What are the new trends in tourism marketing?

AI assistants as a new discovery channel, long-term creator partnerships, hybrid FAM trips, film and branded content, source-market activations, measurement that links awareness to bookings and arrivals, and evidence-backed sustainability claims.

What are the biggest travel trends for 2027?

Travel trends 2027 build on current habits: AI-assisted planning, video and creator inspiration, and online booking.

How can destinations attract Indian travellers in 2027?

Segment the market, use regional-language creators, keep a trade presence and create content AI assistants can surface.
